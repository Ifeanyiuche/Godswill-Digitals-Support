Nobody mentions this 75% drop at your checkup
It starts in your thirties, while you sleep.

Hi {first name},

Think back to the way you slept at twenty-two.

You probably fell asleep within minutes, slept through thunderstorms and woke up feeling as though somebody had swapped your batteries overnight.

Most of that came from one stage of sleep.

Scientists call it slow-wave sleep, and most of us call it deep sleep.

During that stage your body releases most of its growth hormone, the hormone that helps you hold on to muscle and keep fat in check.

In 2000, a team led by Eve Van Cauter at the University of Chicago published a study in the Journal of the American Medical Association.

They had followed the sleep of 149 healthy men between the ages of 16 and 83, and two of their numbers stopped me cold.

Before age 25, deep sleep made up nearly 20 percent of a normal night.

After 35, it made up less than 5 percent.

Growth hormone fell by about 75 percent over the same years.

The men were sleeping just as many hours as before.

They were simply getting far less of the sleep that does the repair work.

The researchers also pointed out that growth hormone deficiency goes hand in hand with more body fat, less muscle and a lower capacity for exercise.

Now think about what happened to your weight somewhere between thirty-five and fifty.

You probably blamed the usual suspects: a slower metabolism, less time to exercise, a few too many evenings with a glass of wine.

They all play a part.

Almost nobody is told that the night itself changed, years before the scale did.

You can't go back to sleeping like a twenty-two-year-old.

You can give your body better conditions for reaching the deep sleep it still has, and the page below explains how.
