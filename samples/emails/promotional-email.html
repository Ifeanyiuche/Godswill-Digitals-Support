Hi {first name},

Daylight saving time ends in two weeks.

For most of us, that means the evening walk will happen in the dark until spring.

If your dog pulls, the dark changes things more than you might expect.

Federal crash data shows that about three quarters of pedestrian deaths in the US happen after dark.

At night you cannot see the cracked sidewalk, the cyclist without a light or the cat under the parked car, but your dog can, and he usually sees it a second before you do.

That is often the moment he lunges, while you are holding the leash in one hand and your phone flashlight in the other.

People get hurt this way more often than you would think.

A 2019 study in JAMA Surgery found that emergency room visits for fractures among Americans aged 65 and over linked to walking a leashed dog rose from 1,671 in 2004 to 4,396 in 2017, and more than three quarters of those injuries happened to women.

Dogs pay a price too.

Researchers at Nottingham Trent University tested seven types of collar on a model neck, and every one of them, including the padded ones, produced enough pressure during a pull to risk injury.

Here is the good news.

Pulling is a learned habit, and it can be unlearned in fourteen days with ten minutes of practice a day.

Start this week, and you will reach the real-walk stage, with other dogs, cyclists and the park gate, right as the evenings turn dark.

  • A troubleshooting guide for lunging, reactivity and the dog who behaves perfectly until he spots a squirrel.
  • A one-page cheat sheet for everyone else in the house who walks him.
