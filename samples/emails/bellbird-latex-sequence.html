Your mattress might be the most comfortable spot in the house for dust mites
They do not drink. They take their water from the air around you.

Hi {first name},

Dust mites do not drink water.

They absorb moisture from the air around them, which is why ASCIA links them so closely to humidity and warmth.

Now think about what happens in your bed every night.

Your body gives off heat and moisture for hours, and the mattress underneath you decides where it goes.

A surface that lets warm, damp air escape dries out between nights.

A surface that holds it creates a small, warm, humid pocket a few centimetres below your face, which suits dust mites perfectly.

Memory foam was designed to soften with body heat and mould itself around you.

That close hug is what people love about it, and the same density makes it slow to let heat escape.

Plenty of foam brands now add gel beads and cooling layers for exactly that reason.

There is a second issue that gets far less attention.

Engineers at the University of Texas tested twenty crib mattresses and found that every one released volatile organic compounds.

At body temperature, emissions were about two and a half times higher than at room temperature, polyurethane foam released the widest range of compounds, and the air in the sleeper's breathing zone carried more of them than the rest of the room.

We designed Bellbird around what ASCIA recommends.

The core is natural latex made from rubber tree sap, baked with pinholes that let air move through it each time you shift.

The cover zips off and washes at 60°C, the temperature ASCIA recommends for killing mites.

Every mattress also comes with a dust mite resistant protector.

No mattress can promise to eliminate dust mites, and we will not claim ours does.

A cooler, drier bed that you can wash hot gives them far less to work with.

People hate 6 things about latex mattresses, and we kept every one

Hi {first name},

Latex has a reputation, and I would rather talk about it openly than pretend it does not exist.

Here are the six complaints we hear most, and why we kept every one of them.

  1. People say it is heavy, and they are right.

    A queen weighs around 40 kilograms.

    Dense natural latex stops a mattress from sagging into a body-shaped hollow, and a heavy bed stays put on its base.

    We carry it in and set it up, so your back never meets it.

  2. People say it costs more.

    Up front, it does.

    The Sleep Foundation puts the average life of a latex mattress at 7.5 to 8.5 years against 6 to 7 for memory foam, and ours carries a 15-year warranty.

    Over ten years a queen works out at about 60 cents a night.

  3. People say it feels bouncy.

    Latex pushes back when you move, so rolling over never feels like climbing out of a hole.

    Side sleepers with sore hips often find that responsiveness is exactly what they were missing.

  4. People say it smells like rubber at first.

    For a few days it can, because natural rubber comes from tree sap.

    We air every core for two weeks before it ships, and you will always know exactly what you are smelling.

  5. People ask about latex allergy, and they should.

    The proteins behind latex allergy are largely washed out during manufacturing, and the core stays sealed inside the cover and protector.

    If you have a diagnosed latex allergy, please speak to your doctor or allergist before buying any latex product.

  6. People say it is too firm.

    Bellbird comes in soft, medium and firm, the top layer zips out, and if the feel is not right during your trial we swap the layer for free.
