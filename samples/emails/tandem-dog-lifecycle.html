Welcome to Tandem

A back-clip harness can teach a dog to pull even harder.

You know the moment.

You open the front door, your dog hits the end of the leash like he's been fired out of a cannon, and you're jogging down the street before you've even locked up.

Most harnesses clip on the back, which seems sensible.

When a dog feels pressure pulling him backwards, though, his instinct is to lean into it, the same instinct sled dogs rely on.

A back clip gives him something to push against.

Collars bring their own problem.

Researchers at Nottingham Trent University tested seven kinds of collars on a model neck, and every one, including the padded ones, produced enough pressure during a pull to risk injury.

They recommend a harness that keeps pressure off the neck.

The Tandem Harness has a front ring on the chest.

When he surges, the leash turns him gently back towards you instead of giving him something to lean on.

Get the size right

We only need two numbers from you.

1
Measure his chest girth.
Wrap the tape around the widest part of his ribcage, just behind the front legs.
2
Measure his neck.
Measure where a collar would sit, snug but not tight.
3
Check the two-finger rule once it's on.
Two fingers should slide under every strap. If more fit, he can back out of it.
