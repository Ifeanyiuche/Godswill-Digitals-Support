Write your sales page before you build the product
It sounds backwards, and it saves weeks.

Hi {first name},

This week I want to share the single habit that has saved me the most time on every product I have made.

I write the sales page before I build anything.

It sounds backwards, so let me explain why it works.

When you write a sales page, you are forced to answer three questions in plain words.

Who is this for?

What will they be able to do when they finish?

Why should they believe it will work?

If you cannot answer those questions on a page, you will not be able to answer them in the product either, and you will keep adding material in the hope that the answers appear.

Here is the exercise, and it takes about forty-five minutes.

  1. Write a headline that names the reader and the result, in one sentence.
  2. Write three short paragraphs describing their problem in the words they would use to describe it to a friend.
  3. List only the parts of your product that directly produce the result in your headline.
  4. Cross out anything on your build list that does not appear on that page.

That last step does most of the work.

Most people find they can cut a third of what they were planning to build, and the product gets better, because everything left in it serves the promise.

Keep the page in a drawer while you build, and read it every Monday.

It will stop the scope from creeping back in.

Maya
