Your dog is not being stubborn. He genuinely does not know what you are asking him to do.

Pulling is not defiance and it is not dominance. It is the most reliable thing that has ever worked for him, repeated a few thousand times.

You know the walk I mean.

You brace before you open the door. Your shoulder aches by the end of the street. You have started going out at odd hours to avoid other dogs, and somewhere along the way the walk stopped being the good part of the day for either of you.

And the advice you have been given is a mess. One person says be firmer. Another says use a harness. Someone at the park mentioned a trainer who uses a collar you did not much like the look of.

Here is the thing nobody explained. Every walk you have taken so far has been a training session. You have just been accidentally teaching the opposite of what you wanted.

He pulls, and the front door gets closer. From his side, it works perfectly.

The mechanism, in one paragraph

Dogs repeat whatever gets them what they want. Your dog wants to reach the lamp post, so he leans into the harness, and you follow, because the alternative is standing still on a wet pavement for ten minutes. He reaches the lamp post. The behaviour paid out.

Do that twice a day for two years and you have run roughly fifteen hundred successful repetitions of the exact behaviour you are trying to stop. He is not ignoring you. He learned exactly what you taught him, which is that tension on the lead makes the world come closer.

Which means the fix is not more force. It is changing what pulling produces, and giving him something else that works better.

What keeps failing

  • Yanking the lead back, which he braces against by reflex
  • Buying a different harness and hoping the equipment thinks for him
  • Correcting the pull after it has already been rewarded
  • Practising only on real walks, where there is far too much going on

What actually shifts it

  • Making tension on the lead stop the walk, every single time, without drama
  • Paying attention the moment the lead goes slack, before he tests it again
  • Teaching the position somewhere boring first, then adding distractions slowly
  • Ten focused minutes a day, which beats an hour of frustrated improvising

Why this gets harder rather than easier

Every walk between now and whenever you deal with it adds more repetitions to the habit you are trying to undo. Pulling also tends to bring friends along. Dogs who tow their owners around are the ones who start lunging at other dogs, because frustration on a tight lead is where a lot of reactivity begins.

Fourteen days of ten minutes is a small thing now. It is a considerably larger thing after another year of practice in the wrong direction.

What owners ask

My dog is seven. Is he too old?

No. Older dogs often move faster through this because they settle into a routine more readily. Puppies are more enthusiastic and considerably more distractible.

He is a big dog and he is stronger than me.

Then the indoor stage matters even more, because you are not out-muscling him and you should not try. The method works on teaching rather than restraining, which is the only approach that holds up when the dog outweighs you.

Do I need special equipment?

A flat collar or an ordinary harness, a standard lead and some food he likes. No prong collars, no slip leads and no electronic anything.

What if he lunges at other dogs?

The troubleshooting guide covers it, though reactivity with a bite history needs a behaviourist in person rather than any video course. That is said plainly inside as well.
