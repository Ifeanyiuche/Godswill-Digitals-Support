If your dog drags you down the street the moment the lead clips on, he is not being stubborn. He genuinely does not know what you are asking him to do. And here is why...

Pulling is not defiance, and it is not dominance. It is the most reliable thing that has ever worked for him, repeated a few thousand times.

Why he pulls

What your dog has learned from two years of walks

Dogs repeat whatever gets them what they want.

Your dog wants to reach the lamp post, so he leans into the harness, and you follow, because the alternative is standing still on a wet sidewalk for ten minutes.

He reaches the lamp post.

The behavior paid out.

Do that twice a day for two years, and you have run roughly fifteen hundred successful repetitions of the exact behavior you are trying to stop.

He is not ignoring you.

He learned exactly what you taught him, which is that tension on the lead makes the world come closer.

So the fix is not more force.

The fix is changing what pulling produces, and giving him something else that works better.

What keeps failing

  • Yanking the lead back, which he braces against by reflex.
  • Buying a different harness and hoping the equipment will think for him.
  • Correcting the pull after it has already been rewarded.
  • Practicing only on real walks, where there is far too much going on.

What actually changes it

  • Making tension on the lead stop the walk, every single time, without drama.
  • Paying him the moment the lead goes slack, before he tests it again.
  • Teaching the position somewhere boring first, then adding distractions slowly.
  • Ten focused minutes a day, which beats an hour of frustrated improvising.

Why waiting makes this harder

Every walk between now and whenever you deal with it adds more repetitions to the habit you are trying to undo.

Pulling also tends to bring friends along.

Dogs who tow their owners around are often the ones who start lunging at other dogs, because frustration on a tight lead is where a lot of reactivity begins.

Fourteen days of ten minutes is a small thing now.

It becomes a much larger thing after another year of practice in the wrong direction.
