The three traps

Why almost everybody stalls in the same three places

I have watched a lot of people attempt this, and the failures are remarkably consistent.

Talent rarely has anything to do with it.

It is nearly always one of these three, and you will probably recognize yours before you finish reading them.

1

Choosing forever

You have four ideas and no way to rank them, so you research all four and start none.

The fix is a decision rule, not more thinking.

2

Building too much

The first version grows into a forty-module monster because the scope has no edge.

The fix is deciding what is out before you start, in writing.

3

Freezing at the price

The thing is built, and now naming a number feels like asking a favor.

The fix is a pricing method that takes the guessing out, so it stops feeling personal.

The six weeks

One task a week, and you cannot skip ahead

You get one task a week.

Each one ends with a deliverable, and you do not move on without it.

That constraint is the whole product.

01Pick the thing

You run your ideas through a scoring sheet that weighs demand against how fast you can build it.

You finish the week with one idea chosen and three parked.

Due: one idea, in writing
02Draw the boundary

You write down what the product does and, more importantly, what it deliberately does not do.

Week two is what stops the forty-module monster before it starts.

Due: an in and out list
03Build the ugly version

You build everything, start to finish, at rough quality.

You skip the design and the polish entirely, because a complete thing that works beats a good-looking fragment.

Due: version one, finished
04Five real people

Five real humans use it and tell you where they got stuck.

You fix only what more than one person tripped on, and you ignore the rest.

Due: five sets of notes
05Price it, write the page

You apply the pricing method, then fill in a sales page structure instead of inventing one.

The templates are included, because a blank page in week five is where people quit.

Due: a price and a page
06Sell it

You connect checkout, test delivery, and send a launch sequence to whoever you have, even if that is forty people and your old colleagues.

Due: your first sale

You also get the finished examples.

Every template comes filled in as well as blank, using a real product built the same way, so you can see what a completed version looks like before you attempt your own.

Working from an example is a great deal faster than working from instructions.
