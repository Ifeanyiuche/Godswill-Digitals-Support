RE: THE CALL YOU GOT AFTER THE CRASH

Hurt in a car accident, and the other driver's insurance company called you within two days? That call was not a courtesy. It was the first move in settling your claim for the smallest number you will accept.

There is a reason an adjuster reaches out early, sounds sympathetic, and asks you to give a recorded statement while you are still sore and still shaken.

Nobody plans for the week after a crash.

You are dealing with a car you cannot drive, a job that expects you back, and a body that hurts in places it did not hurt on Monday.

Somewhere in the middle of all that, a polite person from the insurance company calls and says they want to get this sorted quickly for you.

That call is not a courtesy.

It is the first step in a process designed to close your claim for the smallest number you will accept, before anyone knows what your injury is going to cost.

The adjuster on the line is usually a perfectly decent person.

But they are paid by a company whose profit depends on the gap between what a claim is worth and what it settles for, and they are very good at their job.

What happens over the next few weeks follows a pattern, and once you see it, you cannot unsee it.

Their offer arrives before your diagnosis does. That is the whole strategy.

§ 1  THE PATTERN

Why the first offer is almost always the lowest one

A soft tissue injury does not announce itself on day three.

Neither does a disc problem, a concussion, or the shoulder that seems fine until you try to lift something six weeks later.

Insurers know that timeline far better than you do.

So the offer arrives while your medical picture is still incomplete, and once you sign, the claim is finished.

You cannot reopen it because the injury turned out to be worse than anyone realized.

DAY 1 TO 3

The friendly call

An adjuster asks how you are and requests a recorded statement.

Anything you say about feeling alright, or about the accident being partly your fault, goes on the record permanently.

WEEK 1 TO 2

The quick offer

A settlement appears, usually enough to cover the car and a couple of medical bills.

It arrives while you are worried about money, which is exactly when it is hardest to refuse.

WEEK 6 ONWARD

The injury shows itself

The pain that seemed manageable turns into physical therapy, an MRI or time off work.

By then, the release you signed has closed the claim for good.

§ 2  THE DEADLINE MOST PEOPLE MISS

A 14-day clock starts the moment you crash

There is a rule in Florida's no-fault insurance law that catches a lot of people out.

To use your personal injury protection benefits, you generally need to get initial medical treatment within 14 days of the accident.

Plenty of people wait to see whether the stiffness settles down on its own.

By the time they decide it has not, the window has closed.

Day 1 is the crash. Day 14 is the last day for initial treatment under PIP rules.

The longer deadline changed recently too.

Since the 2023 civil justice reforms, most negligence claims must be filed within two years of the crash, down from four.

Two years sounds like plenty of time until you remember how long the injury takes to show itself.

And the evidence is getting worse the whole time the clock runs.

Dashcam footage is overwritten, witnesses move away, and the link between the crash and your symptoms gets harder to prove the longer you wait to be examined.

§ 3  THE REVIEW

What happens on a twenty-minute call with us

The review is a conversation, and nothing about it commits you to anything.

It takes about twenty minutes, and it happens on the phone.

  1. We look at the police report, the photographs and whatever the other driver's insurer has already put in writing.
  2. We work out which costs are already visible and which ones typically appear later in an injury like yours.
  3. We tell you what we think the claim is really worth, including the parts the current offer ignores.
  4. We tell you plainly if you do not need a lawyer, which happens more often than you would expect.

When you probably do not need us

If nobody was hurt, the other insurer has accepted fault, and the offer covers the repair, take the money and get on with your week.

We will tell you that on the call, and it will not cost you anything.

Where it matters is when someone is injured, when fault is being argued about, or when the number you have been offered arrived suspiciously fast.

In those cases, the difference between handling it yourself and having someone handle it can be measured in years of medical costs.

§ 6  QUESTIONS

What people ask us before they call

I already gave a recorded statement. Is it too late?

It is not too late.

A statement makes some things harder, but it does not end a claim.

Tell us what you said, and we will work out what it changes.

I think the accident was partly my fault.

The law still allows you to recover when fault is shared, as long as you are not found more than half responsible.

Your share is often smaller than you assume, because people routinely take on blame that the evidence does not support.

That makes what you say in the first few days even more important.

Will this end up in court?

Most cases settle.

Insurers tend to move when a firm is clearly prepared to file, which is the practical reason having a lawyer changes the number.

How long does it take?

Straightforward claims often resolve in a few months.

Serious injuries take longer, because settling before your medical picture is complete is how people get shortchanged.
