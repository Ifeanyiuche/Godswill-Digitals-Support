Road accident claims, Florida

The insurance company called you within two days. They are not usually that quick about anything.

There is a reason an adjuster reaches out early, sounds sympathetic, and asks you to give a recorded statement while you are still sore and still shaken.

Nobody plans for the week after a crash.

You are dealing with a car you cannot drive, a job that expects you back, and a body that hurts in places it did not hurt on Monday. Somewhere in the middle of that, a polite person from the insurance company rings and says they want to get this sorted quickly for you.

That call is not a courtesy. It is the first step in a process designed to close your claim for the smallest number you will accept, before anyone knows what your injury is actually going to cost.

I am not saying the adjuster is a bad person. Most of them are perfectly decent. But they are paid by a company whose profit depends on the gap between what a claim is worth and what it settles for, and they are very good at their job.

Their offer arrives before your diagnosis does. That is the whole strategy.

Why early offers are almost always low

A soft tissue injury does not announce itself on day three. Neither does a disc problem, a concussion, or the shoulder that seems fine until you try to lift something six weeks later.

Insurers know the timeline better than you do. So the offer arrives while your medical picture is still incomplete, and once you sign, it is finished. You cannot reopen a claim because the injury turned out to be worse than anyone realised.

Day 1 to 3

The friendly call

An adjuster asks how you are and requests a recorded statement. Anything you say about feeling alright, or about the accident being partly your fault, goes on the record permanently.

Week 1 to 2

The quick offer

A settlement appears, usually enough to cover the car and a couple of medical bills. It arrives while you are worried about money, which is exactly when it is hardest to refuse.

Week 6 onwards

The injury shows itself

The pain that seemed manageable turns into physiotherapy, or an MRI, or time off work. By then the release you signed has closed the claim for good.

When you probably do not need us

If nobody was hurt, the other insurer has accepted fault, and the offer covers the repair, take the money and get on with your week. We will tell you that on the call and it will not cost you anything.

Where it matters is when someone is injured, when fault is being argued about, or when the number you have been offered arrived suspiciously fast. Those are the cases where the difference between handling it yourself and having someone handle it is measured in years of medical costs.

The clock is the part people miss

Florida gives you a limited window to file. Evidence gets worse the whole time it is running. Dashcam footage is overwritten, witnesses move, and the link between the crash and your symptoms gets harder to prove the longer you wait to be examined.

What people ask before they call

I already gave a recorded statement. Is it too late?

No. A statement makes some things harder, but it does not end a claim. Tell us what you said and we will work out what it changes.

I think the accident was partly my fault.

Florida law allows for shared fault, and your share is often smaller than you assume. People routinely take on blame that the evidence does not support.

Will this end up in court?

Most cases settle. Insurers move when a firm is clearly prepared to file, which is the practical reason having a lawyer changes the number.

How long does it take?

Straightforward claims often resolve in a few months. Serious injuries take longer, because settling before your medical picture is complete is how people get short-changed.
