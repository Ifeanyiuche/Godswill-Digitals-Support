Why this usually goes wrong

Most companies do not have a marketing problem. They have a handoff problem.

The strategy lives with a consultant. The copy comes from a freelancer. The funnel is a developer's job. Deliverability belongs to nobody until the open rate falls off a cliff. Four people, four invoices, and not one of them able to see the whole thing.

Writing

Copy that respects the reader's intelligence.

A reader will forgive you for asking them to spend money. They will not forgive you for insulting them. Four leads, four markets.

Long-form sales page, supplement
"I was fifty-four when a friend of mine, a doctor, suggested I start keeping a spare pair of underwear in the car. He was not being unkind about it. He had watched me leave the table twice during one dinner."
Men do not buy prostate supplements because of symptoms. They buy because of what the symptoms took from them. The lead stays quiet on purpose, because this reader has been shouted at by enough advertising already.
High-ticket sales letter, coaching
"He did ten years in an Indiana maximum-security prison. He came out with a plan."
No money-back guarantee and an exhausted market, so risk got reversed with biography instead. Every competitor's credibility rests on something you cannot verify. This you can check, which is why it opens the letter.
Cold outbound, nonprofit B2B
"$250M fund, women's health, Dec 3 deadline."
Subject lines that look like marketing get deleted. This one looks like internal mail because it is intelligence rather than a pitch. The sender's track record appears later, as the reason she knew about it first.
Value email, marketing list
"What is better than paying Google every month for traffic? Not paying Google every month for traffic. Paid ads are renting. SEO is owning."
One metaphor doing all the work. The reader arrives at the conclusion a beat before the copy states it, which is the only way a conclusion really lands.
